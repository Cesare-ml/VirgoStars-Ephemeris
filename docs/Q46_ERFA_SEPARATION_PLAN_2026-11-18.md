# Q46 correction separation: plan recorded before new reference queries

Compare separate correction **position differences and their centered derivatives**, not the artificially mixed velocity used to diagnose Q46. For each geocentric target Moon 301, Mercury 199, Venus 299 and Jupiter barycenter 5, use JD TDB `2451580, 2451620, 2451674, 2451740, 2451820` and half-steps 0.005 and 0.01 TDB day. The Mercury case JD 2451674 is a previously investigated near-solar stress case, selected deliberately; it is not a holdout and results close to the Sun are not physical-accuracy claims. Request Horizons Q1, Q45 and Q46 with Earth center, airless ICRF, TT approximately converted from each TDB epoch and EXTRA_PREC.

For the same Rust DE441 light-time geometry, use independently supplied ERFA `ld` and `ab` to compute isolated solar deflection and stellar aberration directions, applying deflection before aberration. Compare the two component tangent-plane rates from ERFA with rates from differences of the existing Rust correction positions, using the same center tangent basis. Also compare the sum of ERFA corrections with centered differences of the Horizons Q45 minus Q1 directions. Rates are per TDB day divided by 24, in arcseconds/hour. Report Q46 versus the Q45 derivative separately: a Q1/Q45 position difference alone does not reveal JPL's internal Q46 algorithm.

Predeclared component-wise diagnostic tolerances: ERFA/Rust correction rates 0.0005 arcsecond/hour; ERFA sum versus Horizons Q45-minus-Q1 0.005 arcsecond/hour; variation between the two half-steps 0.001 arcsecond/hour. Limits are not uncertainties or physical-accuracy guarantees. Report every failure, near-solar geometry and floor proximity; do not tune the thresholds after seeing results. The ERFA aberration routine includes an extra small Sun-potential term, unlike the Rust pure Lorentz boost. ERFA and Rust use the same local DE441 geometry: independent implementation/model comparisons, not independent orbits.
