# Ecliptic and radial rate validation plan — before new JPL requests

Scope: the **standard** Rust geocentric apparent true-ecliptic-of-date six fields, no Mobile activation. Targets Sun 10, Moon 301, Mercury 199, Venus 299 and Mars–Pluto system barycenters 4–9. Use Gregorian July 1 at 12:00 TDB in 1901, 1925, 1950, 1975, 2000, 2025, 2050, 2075, 2099 (90 target/date cases), inside the stated ~1900–2100 orientation range. Inputs are the local classic DE441 and its verified SHA-256. Compare to Horizons Earth-centered ICRF airless apparent Q45 direction and Q20 range, with their TT epochs obtained from the Rust approximate TDB→TT conversion. Query seven points at 0, ±0.02, ±0.04 and ±0.08 TDB day. Use fourth-order centered position derivatives at h=0.02 and h=0.04 days, handling longitude wrap and actual representable JD offsets. ERFA IAU 2006 precession / IAU 2000B nutation and true obliquity rotate **each** Horizons ICRF direction at its own TT into true ecliptic of date. This captures moving-frame terms; direct Horizons Q31 uses a different IAU 1976/1980 frame and is not the primary reference.

Predeclared per-component diagnostic tolerances, not a production budget: 0.05 arcsecond/TDB day for longitude and latitude rates versus derivatives of the transformed Q45 positions; 5e-10 AU/TDB day for distance rate versus derivative of Q20 range. Require both half-steps to be within their tolerances; also report how many cases show a sign mismatch for longitude rate and the sensitivity between stencils. Examine direct Horizons Q20 `deldot` separately from the Q20 range derivative, with an investigative limit of 0.001 km/s; do not replace failed direct comparisons with a position-derivative result. Both codes share DE441, so only implementations are independent, not physical ephemeris truth.

Store request URLs, response metadata, epochs, raw response and results in new local files, check input hash before/after, and never distribute DE441. The reference involves printed Horizons angles/ranges, finite steps, approximate TDB/TT and shared 2000B orientation conventions. State all failures, outliers and any unexecuted requirements; include results across all targets and report near-solar geometry limitations. If the strict limits fail, investigate and preserve the original outcomes rather than choosing limits after inspection.

## Follow-up defined after initial Q20 failures

The initial direct Q20 comparison exceeded 0.001 km/s on 11 of 90 body/date cases (22/180 including both stencils). To test the specific retarded-time hypothesis, use the existing Rust light-time diagnostic for Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus and Jupiter barycenter at all nine original epochs (45 cases). Project both the correct light-time velocity and the diagnostic `V_target(te) − V_Earth(t)` velocity on the light-time line of sight. Before running, set 0.00001 km/s diagnostic limits for the correct projection versus the fourth-order Q20 range derivative, and for the incomplete projection versus the directly reported Q20 `deldot`. Record every failure and magnitude. The incomplete projection is not the derivative of the apparent distance and must not replace the correct physical velocity; agreement does not prove Horizons' internal implementation. These cases were chosen after the discrepancy was observed, so they are an investigative sample, not a holdout.
