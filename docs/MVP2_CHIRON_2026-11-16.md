# MVP 2 — Chiron from a JPL small-body SPK (2026-11-16)

**Status: Locally tested against JPL Horizons and the installed `swisseph` 0.5.17.
Data supplied by the caller; none bundled. Not integrated.** Body 15 of the
backend's default chart array.

## Why a new reader

Chiron is not in DE441. The independent source is JPL's own orbit solution, which
Horizons serves as a **SPK file whose segments are type 21** (Extended Modified
Difference Arrays). The core's SPK reader handles types 2 and 3 only, so a type 21
reader was written (`spk_type21`): DAF parsing with strict validation (little-endian,
all segments type 21, J2000 frame, one target and one center, ordered, sizes checked
against the segment tail), and the Shampine–Gordon difference-table evaluation
described in NAIF's SPK required reading. Out-of-coverage epochs are errors; nothing
is extrapolated.

## Data

The maintainer authorized downloading the file. It was announced as "a few hundred
KB"; **the actual size is 3,694,592 bytes** (a 200-year span, 11 segments), which
should have been stated. It is kept outside the repository; the request, size and
SHA-256 are in `docs/evidence/chiron-spk-source-provenance-2026-11-16.json`. Its reuse
terms were not reviewed. It is the Sun-centered state (NAIF 10) of body 20002060, JPL
solution of 2026-06-05 (6026 observations 1895–2026).

## Pipeline

`Body::SmallBody(id)` is a new body identifier that DE441 refuses. A target whose
states come from a caller-supplied provider uses the **same** stages as the DE441
bodies: barycentric state = DE441 Sun + SPK state; one-way light-time; first-order
solar deflection; annual aberration with the analytic Earth acceleration; the IAU
2006/2000B frame and rates; heliocentric and equatorial modes.
`light_time_with_states` and `deflect_light_time_state_by_sun_with` were added for this
(the DE441 versions are unchanged wrappers). In the Node binding,
`engine.loadChiron(path)` enables body 15 (`SE_CHIRON`); without it body 15 is
`E_UNSUPPORTED_BODY` and says how to enable it. A file for another body or another
center is `E_SPK_FILE`.

## Evidence

- **Evaluation** (prototype and Rust): ten epochs 1900–2100 against Horizons vector
  tables, largest position difference **1.9e-6 km (2 mm)**, velocity 1e-12 km/s. The
  Rust test with the genuine file checks that velocity equals the derivative of the
  position everywhere (1900–2100), that the heliocentric distance stays between 8
  and 19.5 AU, and that the **osculating elements at JPL's published epoch
  (JD 2457916.5) reproduce the header values** (a to 2e-6 AU, e to 2e-7, angles to
  2e-5°).
- Core: 4 synthetic tests (uniform-motion segments, selection by final epoch, no
  extrapolation, rejection of other types, frames, big-endian, truncated, wrong tail
  and corrupt records) and the 2 genuine-file tests. All 421 core tests pass.
- **Against Horizons through the whole pipeline** (separations, which are independent
  of Horizons' frame): with Chiron added, 55 body pairs × 25 epochs agree to
  **0.000008″**. Chiron's range agrees to 1e-12 relative.
- **Against `swisseph` 0.5.17** (98 TDB dates 1900–2100; receipt
  `docs/evidence/mvp2-chiron-local-reference-receipt-2026-11-16.json`): longitude up
  to 1.6″, latitude 0.3″–0.7″, distance 2.5e-6; by era 0.3″ in 2000–2030, 0.55″ in
  1950–2000, 1.3″ in 1900–1950 and 1.7″ in 2030–2100. The reference uses an older
  orbit fit, so the difference is largest away from the recent observations; the
  pipeline itself matches Horizons to 8 µas.
- Node: 46 tests pass with kernel, catalog, USNO files, Chiron SPK and the real
  `swisseph` (11 pass, 35 skip without data), including key sets, `rflag` and
  the name `Chiron` in the three modes (ecliptic, equatorial, heliocentric).

## Not done

- Accuracy of the JPL orbit itself (Chiron's orbit is chaotic over centuries; the
  solution is only as good as the observations through 2026).
- Epochs outside 1900–2101 (the supplied file's coverage).
- Other small bodies are supported by the reader (`Body::SmallBody(id)`), but only
  Chiron is wired into the binding and tested.
