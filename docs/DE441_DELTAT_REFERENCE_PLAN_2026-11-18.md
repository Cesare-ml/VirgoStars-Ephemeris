# Historical ΔT reference validation plan — 2026-11-18

The experimental source is `espenak_meeus_2006_long_range`, TT−UT1 seconds as a function of a caller-selected decimal year. Do not alter it into a default policy and do not infer a confidence interval from disagreement between two reconstructions.

Before running comparisons, declare:

1. Compare all USNO `historic_deltat.data` rows within 1657–1972 with the Rust model at exactly the decimal year in the row. Record residual, published `error`, and counts above that error. The error column's confidence interpretation is not established by the file; do not label it σ. Historical observations/reconstructions may overlap the NASA fit; this is not a wholly independent data set.
2. Compare USNO monthly `deltat.data` records from 1973 to the as-of date 2026-11-18 at the calendar date of each record, using the actual Gregorian fraction of that year. Predeclare diagnostic absolute thresholds 0.5 s for 1973–2004 and 1 s for 2005 onward. The former is an approximation check; the latter is a retrospective predictive-performance check, not an uncertainty interval or physical acceptance standard. These direct measurements are independent of the 2006 forecast after 2005. Record both failures and signed residuals.
3. Compare the 2026 Hayakawa–Sôma–Li eclipse constraints at the **monthly NASA decimal-year midpoint** for 1361 May, 1514 August, 1542 August and 1575 May. Check membership in the authors' stated admissible intervals, preserving both disjoint alternatives in 1361 and the intersection for 1514. Evaluate the full corresponding month to show timing convention sensitivity. These constraints are conditional on the reports' interpretation, lunar ephemeris and sites; the 1361 event is reanalysed, while the other constraints add records, and they are not a complete or statistically independent grid. Do not reinterpret them as 1σ or hard truth.
4. For 0–1200 include NASA's source-derived historical standard-error estimates only as illustrative **source-model** context, not independent validation of the fitted 2006 polynomial. For the future, no observations exist; record predictions and their origin/vintage separately and do not promote any uncertainty curve to policy without a defensible reference for its coverage.
5. Compare 22 pre-1962 Jan 1 TT samples (years 0–1900 every 100 years plus 1950 and 1961) with JPL Horizons Q30. Preserve JD, metadata and raw response. JPL says that before 1962 Q30 approximates TDB−UT1 without maintaining the millisecond TT−TDB distinction; the TT/UT1 evaluation-time mismatch must be stated. This is an inter-model difference, not an independent physical observation or an uncertainty interval. Do not use post-1962 Q30 as ΔT: Horizons changes its UT scale to UTC.

Keep source URLs, retrieval date, snapshots, hashes, exact samples, failed criteria and versioned results; no JPL kernel or app modification is required. The numerical polynomial must be evaluated by Rust, not reimplemented in Python. All diagnostic thresholds are local choices and not a claim of product requirements or a statement about physical truth.

After seeing the retrospective mismatches, add an explicitly post-hoc **sensitivity illustration**, not a fit or acceptance test: at fixed UT1 near July 1 of years 0, 500, 1000 and 1500, sample the Moon with the NASA 2004-era tabulated standard errors 260, 140, 55 and 20 seconds, respectively. Separately use nominal 1514 model and the two published 1514 eclipse limits at one fixed UT1 near the eclipse. The first four use three samples; the last uses separate values, not a symmetric error bar. Use local DE441 packs, hash before/after, and state that no interpolation, confidence guarantee, bound on angular extrema, or independent physical validation follows.
