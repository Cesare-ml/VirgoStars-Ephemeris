# Horizons Q46 investigation plan — 2026-11-18

Record before the new reference requests. Aim: distinguish numerical artifacts from a difference of rate definitions or implementation. This is an investigative experiment, not a release-accuracy gate.

- Verify Q45/Q46 columns, Earth/target, frame, time scale, angular-rate units, RA wrapping and cos(dec) against the raw JPL response and documentation. Both comparisons use the same DE441 and geocentric, airless ICRF apparent direction; engine JD TDB and JPL JD TT differ by an approximate conversion.
- Recompute existing offline rate-component variants for all 610 previously obtained Q46 rows. The reference and variants are not new holdouts: these cases informed earlier hypotheses. Test whether omitting derivatives of retarded emission time, stellar aberration and solar deflection accounts for the vector mismatch. Deliberately mixed states are diagnostics only, not valid apparent velocities. Report all variants and both components, not just the best.
- At the six cases in the existing consistency script, query new Q45 and Q46 values with symmetric half-steps 0.0005, 0.002 and 0.01 TDB day. Predeclare the stability check: if the Q45 difference changes by less than 0.005 arcsecond/hour across these steps while Q46 remains more than 0.01 arcsecond/hour away, the discrepancy cannot reasonably be attributed to that choice of step alone. This threshold is diagnostic, not a statistical confidence bound.
- If Horizons supports it, additionally compare at the same epochs with a solar-system-barycentric observer, whose barycentric observer velocity and acceleration are zero. State changes in observer and solar-deflection conventions explicitly. The expected pattern under the candidate hypothesis is that eliminating observer acceleration reduces that contribution but emission-time effects may remain. Any changed center changes the observed geometry and does not uniquely isolate an internal Horizons algorithm.
- Preserve raw responses, epoch, checksums and command lines locally; avoid redistributing the kernel. Distinguish observed reproducible differences and their explanatory model from any claim about unpublished Horizons internals.

## Prespecified modern Mercury holdout

After examining the selected year-0 barycentric Mercury case, request five previously unused Mercury epochs: July 1 at 12:00 TDB in years 1991, 2003, 2017, 2023 and 2031. The centered Q45 half-step is 0.002 TDB day, with 0.0005 and 0.01 day as sensitivity checks. Compare SSB-observer Q46 with the Rust light-time-only hybrid and Q45's derivative with the Rust rate including `dLT/dt`; both are projected on the light-time-only direction. Set before fetching a per-component diagnostic limit of 0.001 arcsecond/hour for each comparison. Count every case, including failures, and retain Earth-observer results as context without applying this SSB hypothesis to the different geometry. These dates are a small holdout for this selection, not independent dynamics, an accuracy guarantee or proof of JPL internals.

## Follow-up after holdout failures

The five-case light-time-only versus Q45 comparison failed. Before making further requests, compare Horizons Q1 astrometric (light-time only, ICRF) against Q45 for the same five barycentric Mercury cases and half-steps 0.002 and 0.01 TDB day. At SSB the observer's barycentric velocity is zero. For each case test Rust's light-time-only derivative against a centered difference of Q1 with a predeclared per-component 0.001 arcsecond/hour diagnostic limit, and compare `Q45 − Q1` rates without setting an acceptance threshold. Check Horizons metadata and source for both quantities. This may explain the previously omitted correction contribution, but does not prove the exact internal Q46 formula; do not discard the failed original holdout.
