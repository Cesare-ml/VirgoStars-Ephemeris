# Station and orientation-boundary validation plan — before JPL queries

Use the standard geocentric apparent Rust six-field output, the local classic DE441, Horizons Q45 ICRF apparent direction and Q20 range, and ERFA IAU 2006/2000B rotation at each sample TT, as in the previous rate comparison. Both implementations use DE441; this is not independent physical-orbit validation. No Mobile activation.

## Station selection and tests

For Mercury, Venus and Mars–Pluto system barycenters, scan Rust longitude speeds at five-day intervals from JD TDB 2460310.5 (2024-01-01) to 2461405.5 (2026-12-31). Refine the first positive→negative and first negative→positive crossings for each target by bisection until the TDB bracket is at most 0.0001 day. Roots are **selected by Rust**, not independently certified. Report missing brackets; do not fabricate an event. For each selected event evaluate the root midpoint and offsets −6, −1, +1 and +6 hours; reference derivatives use five JPL/ERFA points with fourth-order half-steps 0.02 and 0.04 TDB day (offsets ±0.02, ±0.04, ±0.08). Horizons receives approximate TT from the Rust time-scale conversion. Roots close to solar conjunction are explicitly marked.

Predeclared per-component diagnostic limits: 0.05 arcsecond/TDB day for longitude and latitude speed, 5e-10 AU/TDB day for range speed. Record each stencil and failures. Compare signs only when the absolute longitude speed in **both** Rust and JPL/ERFA is greater than 0.0005 arcsecond/day; otherwise label it indeterminate, not a pass. Check both sides at ±1 and ±6 hours and count whether the expected opposite signs are confirmed, indeterminate or contradicted. This deadband is a reporting choice covering part of output-rounding noise, not a statistical uncertainty or a rigorous station-time error bound. Also report Q20 `deldot` minus the Q20-range derivative separately against 0.001 km/s, preserving failures.

## Boundaries

The orientation primitive allows TT JD `[2415020.0, 2488070.0)`; the six-field rate requires a ±0.01 TT-day orientation stencil, so do not assume the entire primitive interval admits the six-field rate. Evaluate 10 targets (Sun, Moon and the eight planets/barycenters) at approximate TDB offsets 0.0101, 0.03, 0.1 and 1.0 day **inside** each bound; record the actual converted TT and verify that the internal ±0.01-day samples lie inside the half-open interval. Reference h=0.0025 and 0.005 TDB-day fourth-order stencils keep all JPL/ERFA reference epochs inside the orientation interval, including the closest center; test this condition instead of assuming it. Use the same rate and direct-Q20 limits. Separately test the six-field API at each bound, 0.005 day inside and 0.0001 day outside: these must fail closed with an orientation error. Distinguish valid orientation primitives from unsupported six-field rates; exact transitions are subject to TT/TDB and f64 rounding.

Save raw responses, complete cases and input hashes in a new owner-local report. A priori diagnostic limits are not physical uncertainty bounds or a release-acceptance budget. Do not infer validity between samples, on other models or beyond the supported guard. Do not distribute the kernel or raw JPL data.
